# Who Offline Knowledge Graph Is For

Offline Knowledge Graph is for people who need to **think across connected ideas**, preserve the reasoning behind those connections, and keep the working graph under their own control. It is most useful when a folder of notes, a bookmark list, or a linear document makes it difficult to see how concepts, claims, sources, and open questions relate.

> The app is a personal research and sense-making workspace. It does not replace a regulated record system, a legal case-management platform, a clinical system, or an institutional source of truth.

## 1. The best-fit user

The strongest fit is someone who works iteratively: they capture an idea, connect it to evidence or a counterpoint, revisit the link later, and refine the graph as understanding changes. They value local storage, portable exports, and explicit relationship notes more than mandatory accounts, live collaboration, or opaque cloud ranking.

| User profile | Typical need | What the graph contributes |
|---|---|---|
| Independent researcher | Track a question across papers, observations, and hypotheses. | Records claims, sources, counterpoints, confidence, and unresolved gaps in one navigable structure. |
| Student or educator | Build a durable map of a topic rather than a one-off revision list. | Connects concepts to readings, quotations, questions, and revision prompts. |
| Writer, journalist, or analyst | Develop an outline while retaining the evidence and caveats behind it. | Keeps story angles, people, events, themes, sources, and uncertainties visibly connected. |
| Product, design, or strategy practitioner | Make assumptions and research findings easier to inspect. | Links user needs, experiments, decisions, dependencies, and evidence without requiring a cloud workspace. |
| Software architect or technical lead | Explore systems, trade-offs, incidents, and implementation decisions. | Maps components, interfaces, constraints, failure modes, and supporting documentation. |
| Policy, nonprofit, or community practitioner | Understand a local issue with multiple stakeholders and evidence types. | Relates needs, actors, interventions, source material, risks, and open questions. |
| Creator or lifelong learner | Maintain a personal commonplace book that is more navigable than tags alone. | Connects ideas, projects, reading notes, inspirations, and next actions. |

## 2. Fields and practical applications

The app is field-agnostic, but it is especially suited to work where the *relationship between items* matters as much as the items themselves. The examples below describe possible uses, not specialist advice.

| Field | Useful graph elements | Example workflow | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Academic research | Research question, claim, source, quotation, method, counterpoint. | Capture each reading, connect its claims to a question, then use Evidence Review to find unsupported links. | Verify citations and conclusions against original material. |
| Education | Topic, definition, example, misconception, exercise, source. | Build a course map from key ideas to examples and self-test prompts. | It is not a learning-management system or an official student record. |
| Writing and journalism | Theme, person, event, source, scene, unresolved question. | Connect notes and source excerpts before drafting an outline. | Maintain independent editorial, consent, and source-protection practices. |
| Product and service design | User need, insight, assumption, experiment, decision, dependency. | Link research evidence to product hypotheses and the decisions they inform. | Keep formal approvals and operational records in the appropriate system. |
| Software and systems design | Service, component, interface, incident, constraint, decision. | Create a system map, annotate links with rationale, then focus a neighborhood while investigating a change. | It is not a deployment, monitoring, or incident-response system. |
| Public-interest and policy work | Stakeholder, issue, intervention, indicator, source, risk. | Map competing viewpoints and evidence before preparing a briefing. | Do not treat the graph as authoritative policy, legal, or regulatory advice. |
| Personal knowledge management | Idea, project, book, person, question, habit. | Capture fragments in the inbox, promote useful notes, and connect them during weekly review. | Back up locally; the app does not provide a hosted recovery service. |
| Creative practice | Motif, reference, character, setting, project, constraint. | Link influences and work-in-progress material to reveal thematic or structural gaps. | Respect licenses, confidentiality, and collaborators’ permissions. |

## 3. A useful starting pattern

Begin with a **small, answerable question** rather than trying to model an entire field. For example, a student might ask, “What are the competing explanations for this event?” A product researcher might ask, “Which evidence supports the most important user need?” A technical lead might ask, “Which components are affected by this interface change?”

Create one concept for the question, three to five concepts for the most relevant ideas or sources, and only the relationships you can explain in a sentence. Add a note or source to each important relationship. This keeps the first graph interpretable and makes later filtering, evidence review, and focused exploration more valuable.

## 4. How different users can work in the app

| Stage | Research or study | Product or technical work | Personal or creative work |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capture | Add a source, claim, quotation, or question. | Add an observation, system component, assumption, or decision. | Add an idea, reference, project, or prompt. |
| Connect | Describe whether a source supports, challenges, or contextualizes a claim. | Explain a dependency, trade-off, user need, or effect. | Explain why two ideas belong together. |
| Inspect | Filter by tag or relationship type and open the relevant neighborhood. | Use focused Explore mode to inspect affected components or evidence. | Use tag clusters and recent activity to decide what to develop next. |
| Improve | Use Evidence Review to identify weak or unsupported links. | Revisit assumptions and decision rationale after new evidence. | Archive stale material and strengthen notes on the ideas that remain useful. |
| Protect | Export a complete ZIP after meaningful work and test recovery periodically. | Keep a protected export separate from operational systems. | Save a local copy before changing devices or restructuring a project. |

## 5. Local-first suitability checklist

Offline Knowledge Graph is a good fit when the following statements are mostly true:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Do relationships, not just tags, matter to the work? | The app is designed to show and explain links between concepts. |
| Can the work start on one device and remain useful without a required account? | Core capture, search, exploration, export, and restore are local-first. |
| Is a portable ZIP backup acceptable for recovery and transfer? | Recovery depends on exports that the owner saves deliberately. |
| Can the owner explain and maintain important connections? | Relationship notes and evidence are central to an interpretable graph. |
| Is asynchronous, manual exchange acceptable when sharing is needed? | The app does not depend on a mandatory real-time collaboration service. |

## 6. When another tool may be more appropriate

Choose a purpose-built system when you need simultaneous multi-user editing, enterprise retention controls, formal workflows, regulated recordkeeping, legal evidence management, clinical decision support, or a hosted organization-wide source of truth. The app can still be useful as a private working map beside those systems, but it should not be presented as their replacement.

If your graph contains sensitive information, first consider whether it belongs on the device at all. Use passphrase-protected exports and device security where appropriate, keep the passphrase separately from the archive, and follow the data-handling requirements that apply to your own context.
